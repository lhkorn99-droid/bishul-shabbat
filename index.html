What Bishul is

17:15

Bishul is a significant change to an item through heat. It is not "making food edible." That definition is wider than most people assume in three directions.

Food that is already edible raw

An apple is fine to eat as it is. Baking it still changes it significantly, so Bishul applies.

Raw food

The obvious case. Raw meat, raw grain, raw vegetables brought to an edible state.

Things that are not food

Softening metal in a fire is Bishul too. The melacha is about the change, not the meal.

The stages of cooking

20:23

Four terms you need before the cooking range makes sense. These are the key terms to post in the class chat.

1 · יד סולדת בו

Yad soledes bo

Hot enough that the hand pulls back by reflex. The shiur worked with roughly 110°F as the threshold where warming starts to matter.

2 · מאכל בן דרוסאי

Maachal ben Drusai

Edible under extreme circumstances. A rare steak: someone would eat it in a pinch, so it already counts as partly cooked.

3 · מבושל כל צרכו

Mevushal kol tzorko

Fully cooked, readily edible under normal circumstances. The point past which any further cooking is halachically insignificant.

4 · מצטמק ויפה לו

Mitztamek v'yafeh lo

Cooked past done to an improved state. Cholent that has thickened into a stew is better for the extra hours, which matters later for leaving food on the fire.

The cooking range

29:00

Bishul only applies inside a window. Before the start point, heat has not changed the food enough to count. After the end point, more heat changes nothing that halacha cares about. Solids and liquids have different windows.

Solids

  • Start: maachal ben Drusai. Once the food is edible in extreme circumstances, heating it further is Bishul.
  • End: mevushal kol tzorko. Cooking beyond fully cooked is halachically insignificant.
  • For foods that contain liquid or are edible raw, assume the start point is yad soledes bo (~110°F).

Liquids

  • Start: yad soledes bo (~110°F).
  • End, strict view: boiling (212°F). Until a liquid has boiled, heating it more is still Bishul.
  • End, lenient view (R' Moshe Feinstein): yad soledes bo itself, taken at the higher figure of ~160°F. Once a liquid has been that hot, there is no further Bishul.

The urn with an external gauge

The water in the side gauge may never reach a boil, though it is well past 160°F. When the level drops and gauge water mixes back into the tank, is that Bishul? Lenient view: no, it passed yad soledes bo long ago, so the urn is fine as is. Strict view: it never boiled, so empty the gauge before Shabbos.

Gezeira 1 · Warming below yad soledes bo

1:10:26

Below the start point there is no Bishul from the Torah. The Rabbis still said no to warming in certain places, because of what you might do next.

The rule. You may not warm food in a place that could reach yad soledes bo, even if you intend to take it off sooner.

The concern. You forget, and the food cooks. The decree looks at the place, not your plan.

So the question to ask is not "how hot will I let it get?" but "how hot could this spot get it?"

Exceptions

Each one removes the concern of forgetting, or outweighs it.

Permitted

A safe spot. The heat source cannot bring the food to yad soledes bo, so there is nothing to forget about.

Permitted

A sensitive vessel. If the container would break from the heat, you will not leave it there. The concern is gone.

Dispute

A baby's bottle. Some permit warming it in such a place for the baby's needs; others do not.

Permitted

Taking the chill off. For a sensitive person, a child or someone elderly, warming slightly is allowed.

Gezeira 2 · Kiruv Bishul

1:31:40

Anything that speeds up or enhances cooking of food that is not yet mevushal kol tzorko is prohibited. Three everyday actions do exactly that.

Stirring מגיס

Moves the food through the pot and brings cooler parts closer to the heat.

Replacing a lid

Traps the heat and steam, which speeds up the cooking.

Moving the pot

Sliding a pot closer to the flame, or onto a hotter part of the blech.

Stirring is its own prohibition

Stirring is treated more strictly than the other two, because it looks like cooking. That makes it forbidden even for food that is fully cooked, and even in some cases off the fire. 1:41:04
