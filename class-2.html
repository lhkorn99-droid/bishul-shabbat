Kiruv Bishul and the four heats

Study aid for the second shiur: the urn question settled, the full chart of stirring, ladling and covering, the four kinds of heat, and what happens to food that was cooked on Shabbos.

Source: שולחן ערוך 318 Full recording

Recap: two numbers, two jobs

15:44

The window from class 1, restated. Solids run from maachal ben Drusai to mevushal kol tzorko. Liquids start at yad soledes bo, and on the Alter Rebbe's lenient view also end there. Yad soledes bo itself has a range of opinions, so the shiur gave a practical rule for which number to use when.

  • Use 110°F when the question is "might I be cooking?" The lower figure keeps you safe: anything warmer than that is treated as yad soledes bo.
  • Use 160°F when the question is "is this already cooked?" To rely on a liquid being past the window, it must have reached the higher figure.
  • Each number is the strict answer for its own question. The same spread of opinions is used in both directions, never the lenient way twice.

The urn, settled

18:45

Class 1 left the external gauge as a dispute. This class gave the ruling and added a second question about the spout.

Permitted

The water in the tube. It came from the tank, so it was boiled once. Water that has been cooked stays cooked even after it cools, so letting it mix back is not bishul.
Stringency: pour out a cup before Shabbos so the tube is flushed with fresh hot water.

Permitted

The spout. The worry: if an urn could be emptied to the element, it might be damaged, and someone could come to refill it on Shabbos. Pump-top urns leave water at the bottom by design. Standard spouts are mounted high enough that a safe amount always remains, so they are fine too.

One more lid rule · 46:00

Condensation on the lid

Steam that condenses on a lid and cools is water below yad soledes bo. Putting the lid back drops that water into a hot pot, which is bishul of the drops. So when you take a lid off a pot on the fire, set it down upside down and let the drops evaporate before it goes back.

Four kinds of heat

49:16

Bishul is about fire. Heat that comes from fire, directly or at one remove, is the melacha. Heat that comes from the sun is not, but the Rabbis fenced the look-alike case.

De'oraisa

Bishul b'or. Cooking on fire, or on electric heat, which halacha treats the same way.
בישול באור

De'oraisa

Toldos ha'or. Cooking on something the fire heated: a hot blech, a hot pan, the water in a kli rishon.
תולדות האור

Permitted

Bishul b'chama. Cooking in direct sunlight, like an egg set out on a hot day. Not the way cooking is done, so not the melacha.
בישול בחמה

Derabanan

Toldos chama. Cooking on something the sun heated, like a sun-baked rock. Permitted from the Torah, but a gezeira forbids it because it looks like toldos ha'or.
תולדות חמה

Food that was cooked on Shabbos

1:19:07

The b'diavad half of the sugya. The answer depends on whether the cooking was deliberate, and on how cooked the food already was.

Why no waiting period for shogeg

The usual rule after a melacha done for a Jew is to wait b'chdei sheya'asu, the time it would take to do the work after Shabbos, so no one benefits from the violation. Here that is not needed: when the cooking was a mistake, there is no concern that anyone will cook deliberately to get the food sooner.

The Alter Rebbe forbids the shogeg food on Shabbos itself. That is stricter than the Mishnah Berurah's position.

Already past ben Drusai? 1:43:54

The case: by mistake, someone cooked food further on Shabbos, but it was already maachal ben Drusai. Mishnah Berurah: permitted, relying b'diavad on the Rishonim (Rambam, Rashba) who hold that ben Drusai is the end of the bishul window, not just the start.

Alter Rebbe: disputed. A leniency of that kind may only be used for an issur derabanan, not where the question is a Torah prohibition.
