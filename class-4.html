Bishul achar bishul

Study aid for the fourth shiur, on reheating food that is already cooked: solids versus liquids, what counts as dry, the three places you may warm food, cooking after baking, salt and sugar, and fried foods.

Source: שולחן ערוך 318 Full recording

Two separate questions

8:03

Once a food is fully cooked (mevushal kol tzorko), cooking it more is not the melacha. A fully cooked cholent on the blech may be moved closer to the fire, or from a low flame to a higher one, even to make it fall apart more before the meal. Today's question: what if the cooked food has cooled down?

Question 1 · today

Is reheating it bishul?

Has the cooled food lost its cooked status, so heating it is cooking again? This depends on whether it is a solid or a liquid.
Question 2 · separate

Where may you reheat it?

Even where there is no bishul, the Rabbis forbade warming food in a place where people cook, because it looks like cooking. That is a different set of rules, covered below.

"Ein bishul achar bishul" does not mean "you may cook a cooked food." It means the issur bishul does not apply to a food that is already cooked.

Solid or liquid

12:12
Davar yavesh · דבר יבש

A dry food Reheating is not bishul

Even straight from the fridge, it never loses its cooked status: schnitzel, a baked potato, a potato knish, challah you want to crisp up. Heating it piping hot is still not cooking. (Where you heat it is the second question.)

Q (Levi): Does "cooled completely" just mean below yad soledes bo? No. That is the Mechaber. Under the Rema, a liquid below yad soledes bo that is still warm may be reheated. 26:10

Why does "still warm" help?

36:02

The first two opinions are each consistent. The Rema's line is hard to explain: being warm is not what makes something cooked. The Rabbi found the answer as a young man in kollel, reading the Minchas Kohen while waiting for his chavrusa, and later gave it when Rabbi Metzger of Stern College asked him about this Rema.

Minchas Kohen Derabanan

The Rema is a stricter version of opinion 1. Min haTorah a liquid stays cooked even when cold, so there is no issur de'oraisa. The Rabbis forbade reheating cold liquid because you cannot tell cooked water from uncooked: two bottles in the fridge, one boiled and one not. Leftover warmth is the sign that it was cooked, so warm is enough. It does not need to be hot.

Magen Avraham De'oraisa

The Rema is a lenient version of the Mechaber. A liquid does lose its cooked status, so reheating cold liquid is an issur de'oraisa. To become cooked it needs yad soledes bo; once cooked, it loses that status only when it has cooled completely. While still warm, it has not lost it. 39:34

How we rule 45:23

  • Both readings give the same practical rule: cold is forbidden, warm is permitted. Whether it is derabanan or de'oraisa will matter later.
  • Mishnah Berurah and R' Moshe Feinstein: follow the Minchas Kohen. Derabanan.
  • Alter Rebbe: quotes the Magen Avraham. The simple reading is de'oraisa.
  • R' Chaim Naeh (Ketzos HaShulchan): the Alter Rebbe only quotes the Magen Avraham and does not pasken like him, so even he holds derabanan. Many disagree. The Rabbi: still a machlokes, but a very strong argument for derabanan.

Q (Dovid): Is this why a solid is different, because you can always tell it was cooked? Yes. That is why by a dry food no one forbids reheating, even after it cools completely. 43:13

What counts as "still warm"? 49:00

The Alter Rebbe's words: ne'echal machmas chamimuso, it is still eaten as a warm food. No one gives a temperature. Cholent that sat out 25 minutes at a kiddush is nowhere near yad soledes bo, but you would still serve it as warm food. A coffee poured at the start of the shiur is already cold.

What counts as dry?

50:48

Yavesh and lach can mean solid versus liquid, or dry versus wet. A juicy piece of chicken is solid but wet. Which one did the Shulchan Aruch mean?

Minchas Kohen Any solid

A tray of chicken with some gravy is a davar yavesh and may be reheated; soup may not. This fits his reasoning: the worry is only whether you can tell it was cooked, and gravy on chicken is obviously part of a cooked dish. 53:11

Alter Rebbe · Mishnah Berurah No liquid at all

She'ein bo rotev klal. This fits the Magen Avraham: if liquid can lose its cooked status, the gravy on the chicken gets cooked even if the chicken does not. At the extreme, a carrot and a piece of chicken in a bowl of soup. 54:00
How dry is dry? · 1:00:10

Only the surface, and only measurable moisture

Fat or juice inside the food does not matter; the surface must be dry. A little that oozes out while heating and soaks back in is fine; a lot is a problem. R' Chaim Mirviss: measurable moisture means tofeach al menas lehatfiach. If you touch it, does your hand get wet enough to wet something else, like your other hand or a paper? A faint feeling of moisture does not count.

Where you may reheat

1:05:57

Even a food with no bishul issue may never go on the fire on Shabbos, and halacha lemaaseh the blech counts as on the fire. A tray of schnitzel, potato knishes or franks-and-blankets (if not very oily) may not go on the blech. It is not bishul, but it is the normal place of cooking, so it looks like cooking. A dry, cooked food may be warmed only in a place where people do not cook.

1 · On top of a pot Permitted

No one cooks on top of a pot. Schnitzel or franks-and-blankets on top of the cholent pot. A cold tray of chicken with gravy may not go there (the gravy would cook); if it is still warm, it may.

2 · Next to the fire Permitted

On the counter right beside the fire, even if it gets hot there. But a cup of cold water there is still forbidden: for a food subject to bishul, an unusual place does not help, because you really are cooking. 1:10:34

3 · Into a kli rishon off the fire Permitted

It can still cook, so no raw carrot. But a dry cooked potato from the fridge, or cooked dry lokshen, may go into the hot pot of chicken soup that is off the fire.

Q (Dovid): What about the sun? There even raw food is fine; that is bishul b'chamah (class 2). Still to come: whether the blech is like the top of a pot, and hot plates. 1:12:45

Cooking after baking

1:21:07

Fully baked challah is dry and finished. May it go into the pot of soup off the fire, like a dry potato? Some like broken-up matzah in their soup (not on Pesach). The Sefer Yereim says no.

Bishul achar afiyah בישול אחר אפיה

Baking and cooking are the same av melacha and equally severe, but they do different things to food: boiled chicken versus barbecued chicken, a cooked potato versus bread. Cooking an already cooked food changes nothing significant, so ein bishul achar bishul: cooked, drained noodles may go into soup. Turning a baked food into a cooked one is a real change, so the issur bishul still applies to baked food, even fully baked and dry. This is quoted by the Shulchan Aruch, the Alter Rebbe and the Mishnah Berurah: halacha pesukah.

His second chumra: he also holds that a kli sheni can cook delicate foods, and we cannot know which those are (class 3). Challah is soft and may cook quickly. So challah or matzah goes neither in the pot nor in the bowl, only into a kli shlishi. 1:26:43

  • Even hot challah may not go into the soup. In soup it would be cooked for the first time.
  • Warming challah on top of the cholent pot or next to the fire is fine: that is baking after baking.
  • Challah from the freezer: fine on top of the pot, but wipe off the frost first. Frost is liquid and would cook; the surface must be dry. 1:30:44
  • Grilled or roasted meat: same rule, no cooking after grilling. From the fridge it is usually wet anyway. If it is still warm, it may be warmed on top of the cholent pot, but not put into it.

Baked chicken: a safek

1:32:12

Chicken baked in a pan, at 350 for an hour and a half. It is not cooked in water, but it does cook in its own juices and fat, unlike a barbecue where the drippings fall away. Baked or cooked? A safek, so we are machmir and treat it as baked.

The chumra runs one way. In hot soup, baked chicken takes on a softer, cooked taste: a new effect. Moving cooked (or grilled) chicken into a pan on top of the cholent pot changes nothing about it. A pan on top of a pot is a neutral zone: baked, cooked or grilled food can all be kept warm there. 1:36:50

Salt and sugar: food that dissolves

1:43:12

Chozer v'nimoach: a cooked, dry food that melts into liquid. Mined salt was never cooked (the Rabbi once chiseled a piece off the salt mountains in Eretz Yisrael). Sea salt goes through cooking or evaporation, so it is cooked and dry. Is it still a dry food once it dissolves?

Lenient view

It is a cooked davar yavesh, so it may even go into a kli rishon off the fire.

Strict view

Once it dissolves it is a davar lach, and for a cold liquid having been cooked before does not help. So cooked salt is like uncooked salt, and by class 3's chumra that keeps it out of even a kli sheni.

Fried foods

1:57:15

A drop of oil Baked

Just enough oil so it does not stick, like pancakes. Treated as baked.

Pan-fried Safek: not into the pot

Schnitzel, latkes: fried in oil for flavor. Cooked or baked? A safek, so be machmir. They do not go into the cholent or soup pot, even warm, even with the oil blotted off. On top of the cholent pot is fine (the neutral zone).

Deep-fried Cooked

French fries, chicken nuggets. The Alter Rebbe rules in Hilchos Berachos that deep-fried counts as cooked, and R' Chaim Naeh applies it to Shabbos: ein bishul achar bishul. Nuggets may go into the cholent. The Pri Megadim disagrees: what matters is the medium (oil), not the amount, so even deep-fried may not go into the pot. 2:00:31
Croutons · 2:03:06

Two kinds of crouton

The little yellow soup croutons are deep-fried and dry. By R' Chaim Naeh they may go into the bowl, and in principle even into the pot off the fire. Salad croutons are toasted bread, so cooking after baking applies: not the pot, not the bowl. Make a kli shlishi.

A practical case: lokshen

2:04:47

Dry Pot and bowl

Like the top layer that dries out in the strainer. May go into the pot off the fire, and certainly into the bowl.

Wet, still warm Pot and bowl

Fresh from cooking: a solid food whose water is cooked and still warm. May go into the pot too.

Wet and cold Bowl only

Cooled, or rinsed under the cold tap. Not into the pot: that water is not part of the noodles and is uncooked. Into a bowl of soup, yes: the noodles are cooked, and plain water may go into a kli sheni (unless it is boiling hot, where we are machmir anyway).

Q (Levi): Does it matter whether the noodles go in first and the soup is poured over them? That is iruy from a kli rishon, not covered yet. This ruling was only for noodles put into soup already in the bowl. 2:10:04
